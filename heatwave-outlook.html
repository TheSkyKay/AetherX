How this works

Definitions, assumptions and what would improve the answer.

IMD criteria applied here
  • Entry threshold by region: maximum temperature at least 40 °C for plains, 37 °C for coastal stations, 30 °C for hilly regions.
  • Departure from normal: heatwave when the departure is 4.5 °C to 6.4 °C; severe heatwave when it is more than 6.4 °C.
  • Absolute rule: heatwave when the maximum temperature reaches 45 °C; severe heatwave at 47 °C or more.
  • Warm night: minimum-temperature departure of 4.5 °C to 6.4 °C (very warm night above 6.4 °C), considered only when the maximum temperature stays at 40 °C or above.
  • Not applied: IMD declares a heat wave when the criteria are met at two or more stations in a meteorological sub-division for at least two consecutive days. A single grid cell cannot satisfy that rule, so this page counts qualifying grid-cell days and reports consecutive-day spells separately.
Normals, seasons and data method
  • Normals are the 1991–2020 mean for each calendar day, smoothed with a 15-day circular window, computed from ERA5 reanalysis at the city's grid cell. IMD normals are station-based and may differ.
  • Past seasons use ERA5 daily aggregates. The current season mixes the recent model analysis and the 16-day forecast with the archive, so its method is close to, but not identical with, past years.
  • Apparent temperature is shown as a heat-stress indicator. It encodes temperature, humidity, wind and solar radiation; it is not WBGT and not a personal risk measure.
  • Where daily values are missing, that day is excluded from the count and reported as a gap rather than filled.
What the likelihood means — and does not
  • The seasonal figure is the share of baseline years (1991–2025) that had at least one qualifying day after the same point in the season. It is climatological frequency, shown with a 95% Wilson interval and the number of years behind it.
  • When the season so far has been quiet, a second figure conditions on that: among baseline years with no qualifying day by this date, how many later had one. Small samples are flagged.
  • The 16-day figure is deterministic model output, not a probability. It is not an ensemble spread and does not carry a calibrated chance.
  • No causal claim is made. Differences are concurrent measurements in one grid cell; they do not identify circulation patterns, land use, or any other cause.
Validation design and its limits
  • For each test year from 2005 to 2025, the rule is fitted on 1991 to the year before, then applied. Test years are never in their own training set.
  • Reported: hits, false alarms, misses, precision, recall, accuracy, Brier score, and the same for an always-negative baseline and a persistence baseline.
  • Only the climatological rule is validated. Verifying the 16-day forecast needs archived forecasts, which this page does not have.
  • Per-city results are shown for the selected city; the ranking table adds the same calculation for other listed cities.
What would improve the answer
  • Station observations and IMD's official station normals, plus the district's meteorological sub-division, to apply the real declaration rule.
  • Archived IMD heatwave warnings and district heat-action plans for a proper event definition and lead-time evaluation.
  • Ensemble forecasts for calibrated probabilities by lead time, and hourly globe temperature for WBGT.
  • Local land-use and urban heat-island data, since a 25 km reanalysis cell smooths the hottest parts of a city.
  • Health outcome data (with appropriate governance) if the system is ever to be used for health impact, which this prototype does not attempt.
