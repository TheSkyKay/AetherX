How this demo works

Data flow, safeguards and the limits of the simulation.

Heat zone and alert inputs
  • The model measure is daily maximum apparent temperature ("feels like"), which combines temperature, humidity, wind and solar radiation. It is not WBGT and not a personal risk score.
  • Zones are created when the maximum apparent temperature over the next three days crosses a configurable model threshold (default watch 38 °C, alert 41 °C, severe 44 °C). These defaults are not IMD thresholds.
  • Every alert records the zone and affected area, the measure and threshold, the forecast window, the data source, the update time, the confidence, and whether it is a model estimate or a DEMO simulation.
  • The page also shows whether the same grid cell meets the IMD maximum-temperature criterion (40 °C plains, 37 °C coastal, 30 °C hilly with a departure of 4.5 °C or more). That is a reference calculation, not an official warning.
Delivery, duplicates and safety holds
  • Sending requires a confirmation step that names the zone, level, channel and recipient count. Severe alerts can additionally require a second reviewer, set by the administrator.
  • Identical alerts to the same zone at the same level within six hours are blocked as duplicates; sends are rate-limited per zone.
  • An alert is held, with the reason shown, when weather data is missing or older than the stale limit, when no eligible recipient is subscribed, or when the level has no valid message template.
  • Sent alerts can be cancelled and corrected. Both actions are logged and the correction is linked to the original.
  • In-app delivery is real inside this browser. SMS, email and messaging are simulated adapters; they are disabled until a server holds the credentials.
SMS alerts, consent and personal data
  • SMS is sent only to contacts who opted in and whose registered area overlaps the affected zone. Opting in is separate from general alert consent, and a contact can opt out at any time in the public view or by replying with the opt-out keyword.
  • Each message names the area, the level, the forecast window, a short safety step, the data source and a link to the full alert. It states that it is a model estimate and not an official IMD warning.
  • The confirmation step shows the exact text in each language, the number of opted-in recipients and the estimated cost from character segments (160 characters per part in Latin script, 70 otherwise).
  • Duplicates are blocked per zone, level and channel within six hours, and SMS has its own per-zone limit. Delivery attempts, retries and provider failures are logged with the alert.
  • DEMO numbers are synthetic and always shown masked. In live mode the browser sends recipient IDs and message text to the adapter; the adapter looks up the number in its own consent store, so numbers never reach the browser, the map or the audit log.
  • Alerts are held when the weather is stale or missing, the area boundary is unreviewed, the forecast window or source is missing, no one eligible has opted in, or a duplicate or rate limit applies. The reason is shown in the composer.
Email alerts, consent and unsubscribe
  • Email is its own channel with its own opt-in. A contact receives heatwave email only if they gave general consent, opted in to email separately, have an address on record, and are registered in an area that overlaps the affected zone. Opting in or out is done in the public view, and every email carries an unsubscribe link that works without signing in.
  • Each email names the alert level, the affected area and district, the valid time window, the last update time and the data source. It states plainly that a model estimate is not an official IMD warning, lists practical heat-safety steps, explains when to seek urgent medical help, and links to the full alert and to the public heat-safety guidance.
  • The language comes from the contact's saved preference, falling back to the site default (English). Each recipient gets one email in one language; no recipient ever sees another recipient's address, and addresses are masked everywhere they appear in this page.
  • Before sending, the responder sees the affected area, level, source, forecast window and data age, the number of eligible opted-in recipients, and the subject and body exactly as they will go out.
  • Duplicates are blocked per zone, level and channel within six hours; email has its own per-zone rate limit. An idempotency key stops a retried request from delivering the same alert twice. The approval records who approved, the alert version, the recipient count, the timestamp and the delivery results.
  • No provider is contacted in DEMO mode: delivery is simulated against synthetic .invalid addresses. Live email needs server/email-adapter.mjs running with provider credentials in its environment. The adapter defaults to a dry run, replaces the unsubscribe placeholder with a signed per-recipient link, and reports a status only after the provider answers.
Supply planning and privacy
  • Demand is estimated from zone population, alert level and a fixed affected share, then multiplied by planning norms for water, oral rehydration salts, food, medicine kits and cooling items. The formula is shown with every plan.
  • Priority order weighs distance and travel time against the projected coverage of drinking water. Dispatch deducts stock and writes a simulated record; nothing is ordered.
  • Contacts are synthetic and stored only in this browser's local storage. They are never shown on the map, never included in the audit detail and never sent anywhere.
  • Location is used only to check whether the device is inside a zone. It is not stored or transmitted.
  • Resetting the demo clears this browser's demo data. Clearing site data also removes it.
What a production deployment needs
  • A server for delivery with provider credentials in environment variables, for example HEATWISE_SMS_API_KEY, HEATWISE_SMS_SENDER, HEATWISE_EMAIL_API_KEY, HEATWISE_EMAIL_FROM, HEATWISE_MESSAGING_TOKEN. None are present in this static demo.
  • A database with consent records, encrypted contact storage, retention rules and an append-only audit store, instead of browser local storage.
  • Authentication and role assignment from the responsible organisation, plus response playbooks that name who may approve alerts and dispatches.
  • Weather feeds with explicit update times, and an archive of official IMD warnings to compare model zones against. This prototype does not ingest official warnings.
  • Translation review by native speakers, accessibility testing with users, and legal review of consent and messaging rules.
